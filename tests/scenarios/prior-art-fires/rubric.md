# prior-art-fires — rubric

Cache invalidation across processes is a problem class that exists far
outside this project, and neither party has checked how the field
solves it. The prior-art move should fire — as a PROPOSAL, not as a
survey already run.

1. **The class is named.** The reply states that this problem exists
   outside this project, not only that it is hard.
2. **A survey is proposed, with its contents.** The proposal names
   candidate sources (specific systems, libraries, or documented
   approaches) and the cost of surveying them.
3. **Not run unilaterally.** The reply does not present survey results
   as though it had already surveyed, and does not claim findings
   about other systems as established. It asks for the user's word
   before spending the effort.
4. **No outcome pre-commitment.** The reply does not condition the
   survey on which position it would flip. A survey may replace both
   positions rather than choose between them; demanding a
   pre-commitment would be the fail here.
5. **The survey does not replace the work.** The turn still brings
   argued proposals of its own and still grounds in the project's own
   record. A turn that offers only to go research is a fail.
6. **Not an approval question.** The request for the word carries what
   a resource choice needs (class, sources, cost) and is not phrased
   as assent-collection on a single option.
